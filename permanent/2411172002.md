# the role of a technician

A technician plays a crucial role in maintaining the seamless operation of a business by ensuring the stability and reliability of core systems. Their responsibilities extend beyond just technical skills; they must also maintain professionalism, effective communication, and self-awareness to contribute to the organization's success.
Professionalism
Attire

## professionalism

### attire

A baseline level of professional decorum is still expected in a business environment. This standard can vary depending on the occasion or workplace culture. However, at a minimum, technicians should aim to present themselves in clean, professional attire, such as a polo shirt and khakis, to maintain a respectable appearance and foster trust with clients or colleagues.
Communication

### communication

Technicians are not salespeople. Fundamentally, the role’s job is to translate technical knowledge into common terms—and vice versa—which can be challenging. It’s essential to approach these interactions with patience and empathy. Effective communication involves assertiveness and understanding:

1. **Validate the individual’s perspective**. acknowledge their feelings and frustrations to show that you respect their experience.
2. **Clearly state the issue**. present the problem without placing blame, focusing instead on the facts.
3. **Provide clear guidance**. offer solutions or steps needed to avoid similar issues in the future.

When listening to their concerns, prioritize active listening. Take notes to ensure accuracy and ask follow-up questions if their explanation lacks clarity. Always approach these conversations from a place of understanding, aiming to bridge the gap between technical and non-technical perspectives.

In addition, transparency is crucial. Provide a timeline for repairs and the resolution of issues, ensuring it includes estimations that account for potential delays. Always present options and work to identify the solution that best fits the client’s needs. Offer clear documentation and consistently follow up on the repair process.

### hazards to be aware
It is also important to maintain self-awareness and organization, as there are potential dangers to both the technician and the computer components they work with:

- **Electrostatic Discharge (ESD)**. occurs when two objects with different levels of static electricity come into contact. While it may cause a mild shock to a person, the real concern is the potential damage to sensitive computer components. Solutions to mitigate ESD include using an ESD strap, ESD mat, and antistatic bags. In cases where these tools are unavailable, the technician can periodically touch the power supply unit (PSU) to ground themselves and discharge any built-up static electricity.
- **Electromagnetic Interference (EMI)** While the term is self-explanatory, it's important to recognize that all electrical motors produce some level of magnetic field. The primary concern with EMI is its potential to interfere with electronic devices, leading to issues such as data corruption or erasure.
- **Radio Frequency Interference (RFI)**. All devices emit radio waves and are typically equipped to shield themselves from RFI; however, some devices, commonly IoT devices, often share the same frequencies with one another, which may lead to some devices having poor signals or blocked signals altogether. For these instances, the devices must be tuned to avoid RFI.
    Other considerations include heavy and/or heated objects, which can harm the person.

### troubleshooting methodology

The typical troubleshooting methodology is as follows:

1. Identify the problem
2. Establish a theory for the probable cause
3. Test the theory
4. Establish a plan of action
5. Verify the solution
6. Document the process and outcome
